5. Printing and finishing: what to expect

Podder models are made to be printed, but they are not tested on your printer, material or settings. Before and after printing you may need to:

  • Check and repair the file in your slicer or a mesh tool (for example, fixing holes, merging parts or making walls thicker).
  • Scale it to size. Models download at a generic size; set the real dimensions yourself.
  • Add supports for overhangs, then remove them and sand or file the marks.
  • Drill or clean out holes, such as hanging loops, perches, slots and pinholes, which may print closed or too small.
  • Seal, prime or paint the print. The colors and finishes in the images are only a preview; the model file has no color.
  • Glue or attach parts and hardware, as listed below.

6. Safety

  • Food and drink. 3D prints are generally not food safe. Their layer lines trap bacteria, and many filaments and paints are not rated for food contact. Don’t eat or drink from bowls, chalices, dishes or cups unless you use certified food-safe materials and coatings.
  • Heat and flame. Most printing plastics soften, warp or burn. Use LED tea lights in votives and lanterns, not real candles. Incense holders get hot ash: print in a heat-resistant material and use them on a non-flammable surface. Never leave them unattended.
  • Skin contact. For anything worn on the body, use skin-safe materials and seal or coat the print. Stop wearing it if it irritates your skin.
  • Children and pets. Small prints and parts can be choking hazards. Podder figures and jewelry are not toys for young children.
  • Animals and plants. For bird feeders, birdhouses and planters, use non-toxic, weather-resistant materials and finishes.
  • Your printer. Follow your printer’s and filament maker’s safety instructions, including ventilation for resin and fumes.
